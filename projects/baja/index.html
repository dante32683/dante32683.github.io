Baja SAE

June 2026 to present
My work: Electrical integration and testing, KiCad schematics, Data-logger bench testing, Canvas training workspace, Team website
Status: Schematics in progress, first bench checks done, not yet tested on the car

SRJC is building its first Baja SAE car for the 2027 competition in Arizona. I'm on the electrical side, working on integration and testing: I draw schematics, bench-test the data logger, and work with the other subteams so the sensors, power, and wiring fit the car they're building.

Visit SRJC Baja SAE ↗

Electrical work

I drew the overview, power, and safety sheets of the car's electrical schematics in KiCad, and share the data-acquisition sheet with a teammate. The schematics cover the car's 12 V power, the kill switch, brake light, and reverse warning required by the rules, and the data logger. The logger is a RaceCapture unit with four speed inputs, planned for the rear wheel, front wheel, engine, and CVT. The required safety circuits are kept separate from the logger.

Bench testing

When the data logger arrived, I updated its firmware, confirmed the live dashboard, checked the onboard IMU and GPS, and spot-checked its analog and digital inputs. I also measured the 5 V reference that will power the speed sensors. On USB power it read about 4.69 V, just under the sensors' 4.75 V minimum, one more reason the sensor tests will run on the car's 12 V supply rather than USB.

Team systems

I built and maintain the team's Canvas workspace, which handles onboarding, subteam organization, and manufacturing training. Before using shop equipment, members complete six qualifications: each one is a quiz drawn from a 15-question pool that has to be passed with a perfect score, followed by a hands-on checkoff with an evaluator. I also built and maintain the team website, srjcsaeclub.org.
